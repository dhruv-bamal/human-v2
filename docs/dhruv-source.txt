    Main goals                      Reduce overall body fat and love handles; build muscle and strength; improve stamina; develop a V-taper

Important: Love handles and chest fat cannot be selectively burned. The plan targets overall fat loss while building

1. Plan Overview

  Training status: Returning beginner. The first week emphasizes technique and tolerance before workload
   increases.

  Nutrition starting point: Approximately 2,000-2,200 kcal/day. Aim for roughly 100-120 g protein/day within the
   stated budget.

  Daily rhythm: Meal 1 around 12:00-12:30 AM; 3 km run around 2:00 AM; strength training after the run; Meal 2
   around 4:30-5:00 AM.

  Recovery: Continue getting 8-9 hours of sleep. Seven available training days does not mean seven hard days;
   recovery sessions are deliberately included.
Warm-up and Training Rules

  Before every strength session, warm up for 5-7 minutes: marching or easy jogging in place, arm circles, hip circles,
   leg swings, ankle rotations, 10 bodyweight squats, 5-10 easy push-ups and dynamic mobility.

  Use controlled repetitions. For most exercises, lower for about 2-3 seconds, pause briefly, then drive upward with
   good form.

  Rest about 60-90 seconds between normal sets and up to 2 minutes after difficult sets.

  Stop most sets with roughly 1-3 good repetitions left. Do not repeatedly train to complete failure.

  Most 3 km runs should be easy or moderate. Only designated sessions should be hard.

2. Workout Templates

Workout A - Push, Chest and Shoulders

  Push-ups - 3-5 sets

  Pike push-ups - 3-4 sets

  Wide push-ups - 2-4 sets

  Diamond/close-grip push-ups - 2-3 sets

  Slow push-ups - 2 sets

  Plank - 2-3 sets

Workout B - Legs and Glutes

  Bodyweight squats - 4 sets

  Reverse lunges - 3-4 sets per leg

  Stationary split squats - 3 sets per leg; use Bulgarian split squats only with a genuinely stable household surface

  Single-leg glute bridges - 3 sets per leg

  Calf raises - 4 sets

  Wall sit - 2-3 sets

Workout C - Back/Posture and Core

  Prone Y-T-W raises - 3 rounds

  Reverse snow angels - 3 sets

  Superman pull-downs - 3-4 sets

  Prone cobra hold - 3 sets

  Dead bugs - 3 sets

  Reverse crunches - 3 sets

  Side planks - 3 sets per side

  Hollow-body hold - 2-3 sets

  These movements are useful under the zero-equipment constraint, but they are not full substitutes for rows, pull-ups
  or chin-ups.
Workout D - Full-Body Conditioning

Perform squats, push-ups, alternating reverse lunges, mountain climbers, glute bridges and plank shoulder taps as a
circuit. The exact rounds and repetitions progress through the month.

Recovery Session

Perform 20-30 minutes of easy mobility and stretching for the hips, calves, hamstrings, quadriceps, chest, shoulders
and thoracic spine. Add an easy core circuit only if you feel fresh.

7. Days 29-30 - Benchmark

Day 29 - Strength benchmark: Run 3 km easy. After adequate recovery, test one maximum-quality set of push-ups.
Rest 3-4 minutes. Then perform 3x10 pike push-ups, 3x15 split squats per leg, 3x15 Superman pull-downs, and test
one maximum plank hold. Compare with Week 1.

Day 30 - Running benchmark: Warm up properly and run a 3 km time trial. This is the designated day to try to
improve on the previous 17-20 minute range. Do not perform a hard strength workout afterward. Walk 5-10 minutes
and complete easy mobility.

8. Budget Diet Plan

Use a practical 7-day rotation rather than buying 30 different menus. Repeat the pattern for Days 1-28. Tuesday,
Thursday and Saturday are always vegetarian, regardless of where the numbered day falls on the calendar.

  Suggested daily starting target: approximately 2,000-2,200 kcal and roughly 100-120 g protein. Treat this as a
  starting point rather than a medical prescription; adjust based on energy, recovery, hunger and weight/waist trend.

     Day                 Meal 1 - Pre-training                                   Meal 2 - Post-training

     Mon                 Oats + milk + banana + 3 eggs                           Rice + about 200 g chicken + dal + vegetables + curd

     Tue - Veg           Oats + milk + banana + roasted chana                    Rice + 75-100 g dry soy chunks + dal + vegetables +
                                                                                 curd

     Wed                 Rice/roti + 3 eggs + banana + curd                      Rice + about 200 g chicken + dal + vegetables

     Thu - Veg           Oats + milk + banana + peanuts                          Rice + soy-chunk curry + dal + vegetables + curd

     Fri                 Oats + milk + 3 eggs + banana                           Rice + chicken + dal + vegetables + curd

     Sat - Veg           Rice/roti + dal + curd + banana                         Rice + 75-100 g soy chunks + dal + mixed vegetables

     Sun                 Oats + milk + banana + 3 eggs                           Rice + chicken + dal + vegetables + curd


Days 8-14, 15-21 and 22-28: repeat the Monday-Sunday rotation. Days 29-30: use the menu corresponding to the
actual weekday; vegetarian rules always override the numbered plan.

Making Two Meals Work

  Non-vegetarian days: an economical protein base can include about 200 g chicken, 3-4 eggs, dal and milk/curd,
   alongside rice/oats/roti, vegetables and fruit.

  Vegetarian days: 75-100 g dry soy chunks are especially useful for low-cost protein. Combine with dal, milk/curd and
   grains.

  Peanuts are useful but calorie-dense; do not rely on them as the primary protein source.

  Meal 1 should be relatively easy to digest because the run follows 1-2 hours later. Avoid making it very oily or
   excessively large.

  Meal 2 can be the larger meal: rice/roti + major protein source + dal + vegetables + curd.

  Protein powder is not required for this plan.

9. Hydration, Recovery and Tracking

  Drink water throughout your waking day rather than trying to catch up immediately before the run. Persistently dark
   urine can be a simple sign that more fluid may be needed.

  Protect the existing 8-9 hours of sleep. A 2-4 AM training schedule can work if it is consistent and does not fragment
   or shorten sleep.

  Because you study while standing, alternate standing, sitting and brief walking rather than staying in one position for
   very long periods.

  Track waist circumference around the navel on Days 1, 8, 15, 22 and 30 under similar conditions.

  Record morning body weight several times per week and focus on the trend, not one reading.

10. Four Rules for the Month

1. Do not race every 3 km. Most runs should be comfortable enough that you could speak in short sentences. Save
harder effort for designated sessions.

2. Progress exercises, not just reps. When 15-20 clean repetitions become easy, slow the lowering phase, pause, or
use a harder variation.

3. Track the waist and performance. A smaller waist together with better push-ups, planks and running is more
informative than scale weight alone.

4. Do not crash diet. The goal is a smaller waist while retaining or building muscle. Severe restriction can undermine
strength, recovery and adherence.

Safety Note

  Stop training and seek appropriate medical advice for chest pain, fainting, unusual shortness of breath, or persistent
  joint pain rather than normal muscular fatigue. If a movement causes sharp pain, stop that movement and substitute a
  pain-free alternative.

After the 30 days, the most useful equipment addition for the V-taper and balanced strength goals would be a safe way
to perform pulling movements, such as a pull-up bar or resistance bands.
