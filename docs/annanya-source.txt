 How to Use This Plan

 Your schedule stays consistent each week while the difficulty increases gradually. You should finish most sets feeling that
 you could still perform about 2-3 clean repetitions. Technique matters more than exhaustion.

   Day                           Session                              Focus

   Monday                        Workout A                            Glutes + chest + full body

   Tuesday                       Workout B                            Back + legs + arms

   Wednesday                     Recovery                             Walking + mobility

   Thursday                      Workout C                            Glutes + chest + arms

   Friday                        Recovery                             Walking + mobility

   Saturday                      Workout D                            Legs + back + conditioning

   Sunday                        Rest                                 Full recovery

 Warm-up before every workout (6-8 minutes)

 1 minute easy marching in place; 8 shoulder rolls each direction; 8 arm circles each direction; 8 hip hinges; 8 slow
 bodyweight squats to a comfortable depth; 10 glute bridges; and 8 gentle ankle circles each direction per ankle. Keep the
 ankle movements pain-free.
 Cooldown (5 minutes)

 Walk slowly around the room for 1-2 minutes, then use gentle chest, back, quadriceps, hamstring and glute stretches for
 about 20-30 seconds each. Stretching should feel mild, not painful.
 Progression rules

    Week 1 - Learn: 2 sets for most exercises. Week 2 - Build: move to 3 sets where shown. Week 3 - Progress: add
    repetitions and slower lowering. Week 4 - Challenge: use the top of the rep range, pauses, and harder variations while
    keeping clean technique. Never add difficulty at the expense of ankle comfort.

 For exercises using the 3 kg pair, start lighter or use one dumbbell if two are too difficult. When 3 kg becomes easy, make
 the movement harder by taking 3 seconds to lower, pausing for 1-2 seconds at the hardest point, or adding repetitions.

 uncomfortable.

 Vegetarian Nutrition Plan

 Your nutrition goal is to support training, recovery and gradual body recomposition - not to aggressively reduce food. You
 do not need to count calories. Instead, use a simple plate structure and make protein a deliberate part of each main meal.

    Simple plate rule: At lunch and dinner, aim for roughly 1/4 plate protein-rich food + 1/4 plate roti/rice or other
    carbohydrate + 1/2 plate vegetables/salad. Add curd or milk when convenient. Portions should respond to hunger,
    training and recovery.

 Protein without tracking

 Try to include a clear protein source at 2-3 eating occasions every day: dal, rajma, chole, lobia, soy chunks, tofu, paneer,
 curd, Greek-style yogurt, milk, sprouts, or a combination. You do not have to calculate grams; consistency matters more

 Hydration & practical rules

 Drink water regularly through the day and have water before and after training. A simple indicator is pale-yellow urine most
 of the time. There is no need for sports drinks for these sessions. Keep sweets, fried snacks and sugary drinks as
 occasional foods rather than trying to ban them completely.
 If appetite is low

 Because you are lean and trying to build strength, repeatedly skipping meals can make training and recovery harder. On
 busy placement-preparation days, keep convenient options available: milk, curd, fruit, roasted chana, paneer/tofu
 sandwiches, sprouts, or leftovers from a protein-rich meal.

 Recovery, Technique & Success Rules

 1. Consistency beats intensity. Completing four manageable sessions each week is more valuable than one exhausting
 session followed by several missed workouts.

 2. Muscle soreness is not the goal. Mild soreness is normal when starting. Sharp pain, joint pain, ankle instability, or
 worsening pain is not something to push through.

 3. Protect sleep. Your current 7-8 hours is a strong foundation. Keep evening workouts early enough that they do not
 repeatedly interfere with sleep.

 4. Use clean repetitions. Stop a set when form deteriorates even if the written target has not been reached.

 5. Progress gradually. Increase only one variable at a time: reps, tempo, pause, range of motion, or weight.

 6. Keep placement preparation sustainable. On an unusually demanding day, a shortened workout is better than
 abandoning the plan. Perform the first four exercises for two sets each, then return to the normal schedule next session.
